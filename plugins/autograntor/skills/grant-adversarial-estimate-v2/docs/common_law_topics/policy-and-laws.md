# Common Law topic file: policy-and-laws

> **Packaging note (prototype, not part of the Common Law).** Advocacy and policy organisations, law rows, c3/c4, electoral work, non-US actors. Ruling text is copied unchanged from the published Common Law (version 2026-10-18.2). Which ruling goes in which topic file is the packager's guess.

### CL-45: Ladder rows for successive upgrades of one jurisdiction's law [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]

**Rule:** When a jurisdiction's law can be strengthened in stages (e.g. audits → regulator → emergency authority), price each rung as its increment over the previous rung, give it a probability of being reached by 2030, and credit every rung reached. Fold the expected use of an authority into the rung that creates it (CL-5), not into a separate "use" row. Check the ladder's sum against the nearest federal entry as a stated fraction, and keep each rung at or below the price of a comparable whole law elsewhere (CL-6). **Why:** Lump rows credit nothing for partial progress and hide where the value is; a separate use row double-counts the authority.

### CL-47: Grantee-organised candidate fundraising and electoral work [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]

**Rule:** Credit relationship value from candidate fundraising only through the laws and executive actions it makes likelier (CL-5), never as a separate access row. Price partisan-coding risk as a signed exposure row (CL-40) on the "AI risk concern becomes bipartisan" entry, scaling it up only for the tranche that buys electoral or independent-expenditure capacity. Put integrity and trust-norm harm in a CL-11 branch. Where the applicant offers a restricted "no elections" account, price that option and report it to the grantmaker. **Why:** Access double-counts with the laws; the integrity question divides evaluators and should not be silently resolved; the restricted account turns a values question into a choice.

### CL-48: c4 vs. c3 dollars [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]

**Rule:** Value a restricted-type dollar (c4) by what it buys inside the grantee at that tranche's marginal factor. Do not multiply by the applicant's c3:c4 indifference ratio, and do not raise the CL-4 money price for c4 money: both measure fundraising scarcity, i.e. the replacement value of money, which the grading criterion excludes. **Why:** Applying a 4:1 or 6:1 ratio would multiply the grant 4–6× on an argument the human user has ruled out and would fail CL-9 against the organisation's average dollar.

### CL-49: Survival of state laws against federal preemption [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]

**Rule:** A state-law row's probability is for enactment; multiply by the probability the law is still substantially in force when graded in 2030. Set the loss factor by what the live federal vehicle would displace: ceiling preemption that codifies transparency-level duties costs transparency-type laws little (≈0.3) and stronger rungs (a state regulator, emergency authority) most of their value (≈0.67). Credit the grantee's anti-preemption work in its own row; do not also raise the survival factor for it. **Why:** Pricing enactment only, or using a flat survival factor, hides that preemption bites hardest on exactly the rows that carry the most value.

### CL-50: One ledger, one budget for a paired c3/c4 organisation [Proposed 2026-10-18, by Claude]

**Rule:** When a c3 and a c4 share staff and a program, price one program ledger and divide by the joint program cost for the CL-15 average, with the budget built from a realistic hiring ramp (not the end-state run rate from day one, nor the ask). Treat the grant as buying a tranche of that joint program at the marginal factor for what the restricted dollar can fund. **Why:** Dividing joint program value by one entity's spend inflates both the average dollar and the grant's share; using the end-state rate deflates them.

### CL-51: Leverage factors for non-US national actors [Proposed 2026-10-17, by Claude]

**Rule:** When a US-calibrated weight (a PERCYs M-row, the proposed "member of Congress champion", or a US milestone) is applied to a non-US national actor, multiply by one stated frontier-AI-governance leverage factor. The factor depends on the actor's constitutional role, not only the country:

- **National executive positions:** France or Germany ≈0.2–0.3, UK ≈0.25, EU as a bloc ≈0.4–0.5, other G7 ≈0.1–0.15.

- **Individual legislators:** in presidential or semi-presidential systems where treaty and foreign policy is reserved to the executive, ≈0.10. In parliamentary systems whose legislators shape foreign and EU policy, up to the executive factor.

Apply the factor once. Never apply it again on top of a milestone already priced as a fraction of an international entry, and strip value that flows through separately priced executive rows (CL-5). **Why:** A single country factor either overprices individual legislators or underprices national executives.
